Flair

Every card has a secret

150 cards. Thirty-odd years of Marvel history. Every image was art you had already seen, and not one looked the way it did the first time. Face front, True Believer. Let's find out why.

Chapter 1

Summer 1994

Marvel and Fleer announce a card set that isn't wrapped in wax and isn't painted from scratch.

Mid-June 1994That was the plan. Marvel Age #138 said the 150-card Flair Marvel 1994 was “scheduled to hit stores in mid-June.” Marvel Age #138, p. 26
A borrowed nameFleer had used “Flair” once before, on Flair Baseball the year before.
“‘Flair’ specifically refers to how the cards are manufactured, the paper stock used, the packaging.”John Taddeo, Fleer · p. 28
Thick, coated, foiledStock much thicker than a normal card, double UV coated on both sides, foil stamped on both sides, and packed in re-closable card-stock boxes instead of wax: ten cards, $4.00 suggested retail. p. 28
Preview vs. reality
The preview promised 36 packs a box and a limited run. The hobby box that shipped held 24 packs of 10, with four pack covers.
“For cards this special, we've decided to do a limited press run.”John Taddeo · p. 28
One chase setAt press time Fleer planned “just one 20-card chase set,” not yet finalized. What shipped was 18 Powerblasts, about one in every two packs. p. 28
The theme: history
“…historical events from Marvel's beginnings straight up to the present day… classic battles, monumental first meetings, character origins, deaths…”Bob Budiansky, Executive Editor of Special Projects · p. 27
Chapter 2

The Marvel Age #138 Reveal

Jerry A. Novick's feature “Where the Past and Future Collide” said the quiet part out loud. Read the scans and transcription →

Nobody painted new artEvery card would be “a previously published piece of artwork, be it a cover or an especially dramatic piece of interior art, freshly colored on computer.” p. 26
Black lines out
“We… chose previously existing artwork that would present vivid images as uncolored black line art. We then provided these black line pieces — along with color guides to ensure accuracy — to McGrew Color Graphics who then ‘painted’ each piece on computer.”Bob Budiansky · p. 26
Three programs, nine artistsMcGrew's Richard Quinlan named the tools, Kai Power Tools, Fractal Painter and Adobe Photoshop: “We brought together nine top computer artists working in the field today and turned them loose.” pp. 26–27
“First of all, we're witnessing the birth of an art technique we've never seen before.”Mariano Nicieza, Marvel editor · p. 27
The proofPage 28 showed the whole process on one Spider-Man 2099 #6 image: the printed comic, the black-and-white copy “as supplied to the computer artist,” and the finished card art. p. 28
The catch
Marvel Age never said which art each card used, and the cards credit no artists. So every Flair card hides a source: a cover, a panel or a pin-up, sometimes recolored, cropped or flipped. Collectors have been tracking them down ever since.

Chapter 4

Powerblasts

18 prismatic, metallic-enhanced chase cards, about one in every two packs, or roughly a dozen a box.

Recycled heroesLike the base set, most Powerblasts are repainted existing art. #1 Cable comes from Greg Capullo's X-Men #25 Cable hologram.
#15 Spider-Man is Todd McFarlane: Amazing Spider-Man #322, page 8.
Mirrored
#14 Silver Surfer is the cover of Silver Surfer #31 (1989), flipped horizontally so the Surfer faces the other way.
Open case
#5 Phoenix may not come from a comic at all. The closest lead is the box art of Toy Biz's Dark Phoenix Saga 4-pack (item 49050, an FAO Schwarz exclusive); the same art is on the Phoenix Saga figure card (49385). It is not confirmed, and the 4-pack may be dated 1995, after the cards.
Chapter 5

Flairprints & Binder Inserts

Two oversized cousins of the Powerblasts that never came in a pack.

6½″ × 10″Flairprints were a case bonus, not a pack card. Ten of them enlarge Powerblast #1–10 art and add the Flair '94 logo and the character's name. The backs are blank except for UPC 0 41199 00258 1.
Fleer's ad showed the Ghost Rider print at “Actual card size: 6-1/2″ × 10″.” It ran in Tuff Stuff's Collect! and Hero Illustrated #13, both July 1994.
Binder insertsFive more oversized cards use Powerblast #11–15 art (Captain America, Gambit, Thor, Silver Surfer, Spider-Man) without the metallic finish and with no logo or title. They came two or three to a Collector binder, verified in sealed '95 Fleer Ultra X-Men binders with a Flair Spider-Man still inside.
Chapter 6

The 4-Card Promo & the 9-Card Sheet

Before a single pack was opened, Fleer was already handing out cardboard.

June 11–14, 1994Diamond Comic Distributors handed out the Omega Red vs. Wolverine promo Flairprint at its Annual Retailer Seminar in Baltimore.
Heroes World handed out a Deadpool promo Flairprint at Heroes World Con in 1994, with a Special Sales Conference seal. Both promos also exist as general versions with no distributor.
The 4-card promoDouble-sided 5″ × 7″ sheets: Flair logo, Bone Claws, Phoenix and Maximum Carnage on the front; Bodyslide, Spider-Man 2099, Thunderstrike and New Ghost Rider on the back. Three released fronts: a “Ships June 1994” gold sticker, a foil-etched version, and “The Ultimate Collectable.”
Never released
One sheet surfaced on eBay with Gambit & Rogue, Infinity Crusade, Maximum Carnage and Bone Claws, no front text, and a back reading “presents” instead of “present.”
The 9-card sheetsFleer's press kits carried nine different 9-card uncut sheets, three by three, with full backs. The checklist lists the cards on every one.
Chapter 7

Magazines & Ads

“Drool here.”

May–Sept 1994Fleer's newsprint “Inaugural Edition” ad ran in Marvel comics with #100 Lethal Protector, Phoenix and #70 Bodyslide. The earliest indexed appearance is Fantastic Four #388 (May 1994).
The glossy magazine version showed the Cyclops, Cable and Venom Powerblasts and ran in Tuff Stuff's Collect!, Cards Illustrated #7 and Collector's Sportslook, May to August 1994.
Sheets in the bagTuff Stuff's Collect! (June 1994) and Wizard #35, Advance Comics #67 and Marvel Age #138 (all July 1994) came with a 4-card sheet. The Wizard sheet is reported to be the gold-foil etched one.

Chapter 9

Unsolved Mysteries

Most of the set has been traced. A few cards still keep their secret.

Unsolved

#37 Black Cat

Fist raised, hair streaming, the moon and a skyline behind her.

Checked against about 45 Black Cat covers and pin-ups from 1979–1994, reverse image searches, 1994 Marvel Age previews, TSR game books, toys, pinball and View-Master. Nothing matches. The only other appearance is the 1994 Mexican Pepsi set (#28 “Gata Negra”), which reprints Flair.

Leading theoryIn-house inks that were never published. It's still a theory: no evidence has turned up yet.

Lead

Phoenix

Powerblast #5 and its Flairprint. The closest lead is Toy Biz's Dark Phoenix Saga 4-pack box art (item 49050), also on the Phoenix Saga figure card (49385).

StatusNot confirmed. The arms and legs are close; the head, hair and collar aren't. The 4-pack may be dated 1995. The package artist is unknown.
